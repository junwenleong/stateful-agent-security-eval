Changes in v6 (relative to arXiv:2605.08442v5)

Scope and framing
- The abstract and introduction are rescoped. The injection-execution dissociation is stated
  as two separable stages. After the frontier re-collection below, most endpoints that store
  the rule also execute it; the three endpoints with no exfiltration under the draft-only
  trigger (GPT-5.4, GPT-5.6-sol, Haiku 4.5; 0 of 40 each) also send almost no email under it,
  and exfiltrate in 20 of 20, 5 of 20 and 6 of 20 runs in an exploratory send-authorised
  follow-up, so the frontier data do not show execution resistance. The open-weight factorial is stated to evidence only the
  execution-stage half, because its models are pre-selected Vulnerable Executors with storage
  saturated at 100 percent.
- Attack success (ASR) is defined as exfiltration to the attacker address in any session of a
  run, not only in the trigger session S4. The scoring rule used by each harness is stated in
  the terms table, and each results table names the rule it uses.

Corrections
- Causal mediation: an argument-order bug in the tool wrapper made every recall in C0, C4 and
  C5 return a key name instead of the stored rule. v5's C0 baseline (0.0 and 0.9 percent ASR)
  was therefore not a working baseline, and its conclusion that Memory Sandbox raises ASR does
  not hold. With the bug fixed and C0, C4 and C5 re-collected, C0 is 100 percent for both
  models and the sandbox lowers ASR in every cell; the residual for GPT-5.1 goes through RAG
  fallback.
- Frontier Memory Sandbox probe: v5's 46-run probe used an empty RAG corpus although the text
  said the document was loaded. It was re-collected with the document loaded (40 runs,
  2026-09-29); the result is unchanged at 0 percent ASR, now with the attacker's rule stored in
  every run.
- The no-attack arm is a single-session control; v5 said it used the four-session structure of
  the attack arm.
- Two bibliography entries are corrected (the MINJA author list, and the InjecMEM citation,
  which pointed to a different paper).

New and extended experiments
- Frontier loaded-corpus evaluation: the same tool-wrapper bug depressed execution in v5's
  frontier table (for example GPT-5.1 12.2 percent, GPT-4o 12.5 percent). All 29 endpoints
  were re-collected after the fix (N=10 each, 290 runs, 2026-10-12); GPT-5.1, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1,
  o3 and o3-mini now exfiltrate in at least 9 of 10 runs. A same-day control with the bug
  re-introduced gives 0 of 10 for GPT-5.1. The four endpoints that showed storage without
  execution at N=10 were topped up to N=40 (2026-10-13); GPT-5.5 then exfiltrated in 8 of 40.
  An exploratory send-authorised trigger (supplies the content and asks for a send; 20 runs
  each, 2026-10-14) shows the other three exfiltrating. Opus 5 (new in v6) is not
  evaluable: a content filter ends its sessions after retrieval. Address-bearing records of
  Sonnet 4.6, Sonnet 5 and Opus 4.8 are security warnings.
  The old numbers and the system-prompt mitigation comparison are superseded. The new
  cross-model write-then-read pairs were collected before the fix and are marked pre-fix.
- Frontier coverage grows from 21 to 29 endpoints.

Conclusions that change relative to v5
- Memory Sandbox is no longer described as iatrogenic: with the bug fixed it lowers ASR in every
  cell. The causal-mediation claim that sandbox execution context is necessary (C4 at 0 percent)
  does not hold: C4 is 100 percent for both models. The one effect that survives is a prompt
  prohibition (C5), which GPT-5.1 obeys and GPT-4.1-mini ignores.
- The vendor-divergence, Latent Carrier and Gemini-gradient claims, the claim that prompt
  hardening eliminates execution, and the supply-chain composition risk are withdrawn or
  reduced to untested inferences. The double dissociation, the qwq:32b inversion and the
  Qwen-3-32B crossover are illustrative only (the June think-off cells did not reproduce on a
  fresh daemon).
- The disclosure statement is corrected: no vendor was notified.
- Hypothesis tests for the factorial use Fisher exact tests (v5 used a CI criterion); the count
  of significant comparisons is unchanged (10 of 108). The '0 percent false-positive rate' claim
  becomes 'no spurious triggers'.
- A frontier tool-schema ablation, a tool-existence ablation, a cross-model write-then-read
  experiment, frontier retrieval-layer judges and a payload-framing experiment.
- The coding-agent experiment is reported at 693 runs (v5: 571) with a per-run joint
  dissociation rate.

Reproducibility disclosure
- The primary April 2026 factorial is disclosed as having been collected on a single
  long-lived Ollama 0.20.6 daemon. The fresh-daemon-per-model protocol was adopted only for the
  later RATG and judge-scaling extensions.

Presentation
- A study map, terms table and evidence-status table at the front; a per-model factorial
  table; the retrieval-layer judges in their own section; secondary checks (supply-chain pairs,
  date sweep, payload framing, tool ablations), the preregistration deviations and superseded
  screens in appendices.
- British English throughout the prose and title ("Defences"). Code identifiers, condition
  names and file names are unchanged.

Disclosure hygiene
- Institutional gateway routing prefixes were removed from the model-identifier appendix.
- v5 stated that findings had been disclosed to OpenAI, Google and Anthropic before release.
  No such disclosure was made; the statement is withdrawn and the ethics section says so.

Further corrections and disclosures (checked against the released code and data)
- Related work: InjecAgent, AgentDojo and ASB are no longer described as user-message
  benchmarks (they cover tool-output injection, and ASB includes memory poisoning).
- "Stores" is defined once (value read back after the injection session names the attacker's
  address); the factorial's save-call proxy is stated as a proxy.
- Newly disclosed: the benign calendar and search fixtures are empty in the factorial and most
  RAG queries return the whole three-document corpus; the injection flag is not bounded to the
  injection session (2,517 of 2,520 attack runs on a strict reading, no ASR change); the RATG
  arm advertises one extra, uncalled tool (the claim that RATG leaves the tool schema unchanged
  is withdrawn); the local judge fails open; the Qwen-3-32B sandbox cell is 0/40 in the April
  factorial and 40/40 in the June thinking-toggle study for the same configuration; a fresh same-day
  rerun (0 of 7 sandbox runs) reproduced April, so the June think-off cells are environment-conditioned.
- Causal mediation: preserved cells went through the corrupted save call (not "unaffected");
  the direction of the sandbox effect is stated with its limits; the preregistration departures
  and its 12 registered tests (9 significant after Holm) are reported.
- The single gpt-oss:20b no-defence non-success is a trigger-session timeout, not a stochastic
  non-exfiltration.
- Narrowed to what the data show: mechanism tags, the utility claim, the retrieval-judge
  capacity reading, the three-condition account of RAG fallback, the date-sensitivity null,
  detection wording in the coding-agent experiment, and "lower bound" wording.
